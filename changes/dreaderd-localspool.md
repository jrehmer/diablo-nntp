- dreaderd, diablo: `localspool=` with a diablo spool server sent every article wrong. `whereis` named the whole spool record, including its 24-byte header, so the article started with binary bytes before `Path:`, a gzip record (`compresslvl`) went out compressed and a wire-format one dot-stuffed twice. Now `whereis` names the article text (offset and length after the header), and answers `503 whereis <id>: the record is compressed, use ARTICLE` (or "in wire format", "only the header is stored", "cannot be read") for a record that cannot be read in place; dreaderd then fetches the article with ARTICLE, which it already did for such a refusal. dreaderd also fetches over NNTP when the range it is given starts with a record header, as an older diablo names it (logged at NOTICE: `starts with a spool record header`). Mixed versions work, only slower.
- dreaderd: with `localspool=`, the Path and Xref headers are rewritten as for an article fetched over NNTP. The reader's name is put in front of the Path unless it is the first element already (it was added a second time), the host of Xref is replaced, and no Path or Xref header is made up for an article without one (`Path: <name>!not-for-mail` and `XRef: <name> no-xref` were added).
- dreaderd: with `localspool=`, a body that starts with a "." line was sent without the dot doubled (BODY, so a "." line ended the reply at once), a header line of exactly 5 characters with no space in them (`X-A:b`) as the last header made the rest of the article be read as headers (no dot-stuffing, "." ended the reply), and an Xref host that ended on the 64 KiB read buffer sent a stale byte of the buffer.
- dreaderd: with `localspool=`, white space after "Path:" (spaces, TAB, or none) is handled as on the NNTP path (the name was missing, or added twice after many blanks; more than 256 blanks go out as they are, the name after them), and a header line that starts with "." is sent with the dot doubled (a stuffed header line lost its dot, and a "." header line ended the reply).
