- `parsedate()` (the age check of incoming articles, `didate`, the article age in the reader's log) reads the date formats the Date: headers of Usenet have had: RFC 5322/822 as before, and now also RFC 850 (`Monday, 01-Jan-01 12:00:00 GMT`, with hyphens), ctime and `date(1)` (`Mon Jan  1 12:00:00 UTC 2001`), US (`Jan 1, 2001 1:30 PM`), ISO 8601 (`2001-01-01T12:00:00Z`), `01.01.2001`, Unix time, a comment in parentheses, a few non-English month names, years before 1970 and from 1900. The time zone of the date is applied (it was ignored, so the age limit was blurry by up to a day), a two digit year is 2000-2049 for 00-49 and 1950-1999 for 50-99 as RFC 5322 has it (it was a window of 50 years around the present, which turns 1976 into 2076 and makes 1980 articles read as 2080 from 2051 on), and the age is computed with 64 bit numbers (an article from before 1938 overflowed). Before, ctime, RFC 850 and ISO dates were not read, so they were not age-checked and an article of any age was accepted; with a limit set (not `feedermaxacceptage 0`) they are checked now. A date that is still not read (`banana`, `01/01/2001`, no year) is accepted, as before.
